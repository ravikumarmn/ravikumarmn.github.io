## Problem

Most enterprise knowledge sits in documents nobody can query: PDFs, scanned forms, and long reports. The goal was to make that content usable in two ways at once, as **structured data** that downstream systems can consume and as **answers** people can get by asking questions.

## Constraints

- **Messy inputs.** Layouts, tables, forms, and handwriting vary widely between documents.
- **Throughput.** Collections are enterprise-scale, so ingestion has to stay fast per page.
- **Grounded answers.** Answers and summaries must come from the documents themselves.
- **Healthcare data.** Some of it was medical. Extraction had to be fast enough for clinicians to use. <Todo>Any privacy or compliance constraints worth stating (e.g. PHI handling)?</Todo>

## Approach

### Parse structure before reasoning

Each document goes through **Docling** first, which recovers layout, tables, and text. The LLM then works on structured content instead of raw PDF bytes. <Verify note="Inferred rationale.">That is cheaper and more reliable than asking the model to also work out the page layout.</Verify>

### Agent-driven extraction and classification

An extraction agent **classifies** each document and **extracts** fields into **structured JSON** and a **knowledge graph**. The same content is chunked and embedded into a vector index for semantic search.

### Conversational QA and summarization

A RAG pipeline over the vector index answers questions and summarizes across ingested documents, with **1.5s grounded QA** latency on enterprise-scale collections.

### The same pattern in healthcare

- **Form extraction (Acranton).** A Gemini API–based extractor converts unstructured healthcare documents into structured data in **under 3 seconds**, raising throughput **30%**.
- **Handwritten prescriptions.** Gemini Flash converts handwritten prescriptions into JSON within **5 seconds**, making the data accessible to clinicians.

<Callout title="Parser + LLM vs. one multimodal model" tone="tradeoff">
  <Verify note="Inferred trade-off.">A single vision-language model can read a page end to end, but it is slower and harder to debug when it misreads a table. Splitting deterministic parsing (Docling) from LLM reasoning makes each failure visible at one stage. The healthcare extractors show the other side: for handwriting, a multimodal model is the right tool.</Verify>
</Callout>
